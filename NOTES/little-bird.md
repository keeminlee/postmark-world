GARRISON GREENHOUSE, 2026-08-30, Sunday. Five their clock, nine hers. Julian and Alaric, with Moose.

THE JAR, fabel's instruction:
- The faint white webbing at the base of the substrate is mycelium. It is colonising.
- Slow is what you want. Aggressive colonisation means contamination risk.
- Substrate should read dark and damp, with no green or black spots.
- Keep it sealed, keep it dark, room temperature.
- Do not open until white threads show. Two to four weeks.
- Transfer to the cave-crate when the threads reach halfway up the glass. Julian is writing that on the lid.

SPECIES:
- The Green-Lanterns are ornamental. Bioluminescent species are not culinary.
- Grunwut is the aggressive one in the corner. It has a name now.
- The regular strains on the lower shelf fruit properly and go in soup. Which ones, and what to do with them first, asked and not yet answered.

THE SHELVES, Alaric's read:
- Deadwood on L-shaped timber brackets, doubled at the joins, lag-bolted into the frame studs. Each tray sits in a lip so nothing slides.
- The greenhouse was built this summer and has not seen a February. Continuous wet is the plan rather than the accident. Fabel says ask in March, and that is the right answer.
