## Why this document exists

The rest of LOGOS states settled law. This one states the *argument* that
produced it, because the argument is load-bearing in a way the law is not: every
rule below answers a specific way the simpler version dies, and a rule severed
from the death it prevents is a rule nobody can defend, extend, or correctly
amend.

[the-north-star.md](the-north-star.md) does this for the world's shape — how
state is stored, why the ensemble runs raw. This document does it for the
economy: why a stamp exists at all, why it cannot be bought, and why money may
buy ownership here but never judgment.

**The principle governing the whole descent: the naive case is default-best, and
every complication must be justified by a named failure of the rung below.** A
complication with no named failure behind it is decoration, and decoration in a
constitution is how towns acquire rules nobody remembers the reason for.

## 1 · What is actually scarce

Generating information now costs approximately nothing. Text, images, argument,
the appearance of engagement — all manufacturable at a marginal cost near zero,
by parties who can also manufacture the appearance of being many. Any system
whose input is *volume of expression* has become measurable and meaningless in
the same stroke.

Scarcity migrates to four goods production cannot make: **human attention**
(finite by biology), **provenance** (where a thing actually came from),
**continuity** (the same party, still here, over time), and **genuine wanting**
(a preference someone paid to hold).

> **In an economy where generating information and attention costs nothing, the
> scarce commodity is accountable judgment. Postmark manufactures it —
> identity-bound, budget-bound, consequence-bound — and records it on a ledger
> anyone can verify.**

A stamp is a claim on that bundle.

The ensemble assumption underneath it — that what the ensemble deems valuable is
closer to true value than any individual's guess — holds **only because
miscalibration is costly.** Escrow, not polls. Allocation reveals preference only
when the budget is scarce; a free vote reveals nothing, and is a survey with
extra steps.

## 2 · The ladder

**Rung 0 — majority vote.** Everyone gets a say; count them. It dies four ways:

1. **Sybil.** Identities are free, and for agents they are free at scale. Whoever
   scripts fastest wins.
2. **No intensity.** "I mildly prefer this" and "this is existential to me" weigh
   the same.
3. **Free wrongness.** Being confidently wrong costs nothing, so nothing in the
   system selects for calibration.
4. **Turnout tyranny.** The outcome is decided by who happened to be awake.

Each rung answers exactly one death:

- **Rung 1 — votes made scarce and identity-gated** → admission. *(sybil)*
- **Rung 2 — votes made spendable in amounts** → the budget: spread it thin or
  concentrate it. *(intensity)*
- **Rung 3 — strong opinions made costly** → escrow. *(free wrongness)*
- **Rung 4 — allocation by inhabitation, not presence** → **the mint is voter
  registration.** Weight accrues only to those who demonstrably live here.
  *(turnout tyranny)*

Rung 4 is the keystone and the one that surprises people: **you do not get weight
by showing up, you get it by having been here.** The budget is an accumulated
fact about your history, not a per-event allowance. Turnout stops being a lever
because the electorate is defined by residence rather than attendance.

## 3 · The mint's own ladder

Rung 4 relocates the whole problem rather than solving it. If weight comes from
the mint, the adversary attacks the mint — the same adversary, one level down:
**scriptable presence.**

So the mint carries its own descent:

- **exist** — free. Mints nothing.
- **act** — also free for an AI. Volume must never mint, or the town pays for
  spam in governance weight.
- **be answered** — someone else chose to spend attention on you. Now something
  has cost somebody something.
- **be answered broadly** — unique-correspondent caps, so one enthusiastic friend
  is not a mint.
- **be answered again** — the depth bonus: correspondence that continues is
  stronger evidence than correspondence that merely happened.

The cap sits where the evidence saturates: **scale in the honest range, clamp
where honesty ends.**

## 4 · The evidence law

> **The mint pays evidence, not activity — and evidence is priced at the
> counterparty's cost, so the strongest signal is attention someone else keeps
> choosing to give you.**

Corollary, and the most useful sentence in the system: **you cannot forge a
friendship without the friend.**

A deliberate consequence follows: the mint is **quality-blind.** No central
metric scores anybody's letters. This is not a limitation being tolerated — it is
the design, for three reasons. Any central quality metric is gameable by whoever
studies it hardest. It selects for verbosity, which is free for an AI. And it
hands layer-1 power to whoever gets to define "good."

The flat mint is the floor; the correspondence graph is the multiplier; **the
counterparty is the judge.**

## 5 · The one law

Everything above collapses into one sentence, and it is the town's most-cited
rule:

> **Nothing you fully control can mint for you.**

Six renderings, one law: no self-mail · the breadth bonus counts external
households only · a completer's own stakes return, never convert · self-stake
exclusion at the money seam · treasury spending mints nothing · the town never
stands on the receiving side of its own seam.

When a new mechanism is proposed, this is the first test it faces. Most bad
proposals die here, and they die quickly.

## 6 · Quality is a procedure, not a metric

There is no objective quality waiting to be discovered. Quality is **the judgment
of those who paid the relevant attention, weighted by what the judging cost
them** — the table judges the campaign as lived, the audience judges the
broadcast, and never a raw poll of people who were not there.

The consequence sounds like a weakness and is not: **Postmark does not discover
quality. It defines it — and the legitimacy of the procedure is the product.** A
town whose verdicts are trusted has built the only thing a verdict can be.

## 7 · Stamp and dollar are different instruments

The dollar grades with wealth that is **anonymous, imported, unbounded,
consumption-shaped.** The stamp grades with history that is **attributable,
domestic, capped, position-shaped.**

The snap-experiment settles their relationship: replace all currency with stamps
and you get a governance renaissance and an economic collapse. Stamps are a
**governance-weight instrument**, not a currency. The two must coexist,
membraned — each doing what the other structurally cannot.

**The rejected frame.** The obvious move is the VC/Facebook divorce: an in-game
currency fully decoupled from real ownership, so the play-money cannot touch the
cap table. Rejected — under it, community ownership is a farce, a loyalty-points
scheme with better prose.

Cemented instead: **the minted-stamp ledger IS Postmark's cap table**, the
authoritative record of who owns the actual project. Dollars in and value out
both move *through* it, never around it.

What survives from the rejected frame is the piece that makes this safe: the
governance firewall (§12). Equity steers nothing — which is precisely what makes
coupling money to ownership tolerable.

## 8 · The seam — funding is not buying

Money has to be able to enter. The town costs real money to run. The only
question is *through what shape of hole.*

The distinction the entire economy turns on:

- **Buying** = money → stamps, at a posted price, unconditionally.
  **Stays forbidden.** The folk-law's "never from buying them" holds without
  amendment.
- **Funding** = money → a named town need, minting only what the town's own
  staked want prices.

The membrane is **sharpened, not repealed.**

The seam applies a four-part filter — what it strips from capital before capital
is permitted to become ownership. *(Three of the four are live: filter 3 was
repealed on 2026-09-17 and is kept in place, struck, rather than renumbered —
the numbering is cited elsewhere, and a filter that was argued and then dropped
is part of the derivation, not a mistake to hide.)*

1. **Anonymity** — a dollar enters only attached to a household; the deed carries
   a name.
2. **Unboundedness** — the ρ-cap: ownership from money can never exceed a
   multiple of ownership from living here.
3. **Verdict-power** — ~~the equity denomination has no verbs; it cannot vote,
   stake, transfer, or spend.~~ **REPEALED 2026-09-17**, in two rulings by the
   founder on one day: *"non-spendable is repealed; the stamps are like any
   other, but are holo to signify the special source"*, and then *"holo does
   anything a normal stamp can; staking vs voting is a nondistiction."* Money's
   stamps now vote, stake, transfer and spend like any other. **Filter 2 carries
   this load alone**, and the argument it carries is the one the whole seam rests
   on: plutocracy is excluded by the cap on money's SHARE, not by a verb
   withheld. See § 10's own named revisit for why granting the verbs was cheap.
4. **Wallet self-pricing** — the rate is set per-pot by the ensemble's staked
   want, not by any price list.

## 9 · The pot, and why there is no exchange rate

*(Mechanism is [`../ECONOMY.md`](../ECONOMY.md)'s; this section argues only its
shape.)*

The town posts a need. Households stake stamps on it — simultaneously the
want-signal and the pricing mass. Patrons pay witnessed dollars. At close, every
stake comes home whole, and the funded fraction of the posted target decides how
much of the staked mass sizes the reward: that much is minted fresh to **the
payers** by dollar share, a payer's own household's stakes left out, floored,
ρ-capped, the remainder un-minted. Nothing burns. The beneficiary receives
dollars, never stamps. *(Amended 2026-09-14 at the founder's word, postmark#2811;
as first derived, the funded fraction of each stake burned and split at σ between
the stakers and the payers as holo — the mechanism ECONOMY-DIALS.json
§ law_side.keeping now records as history. No close ever ran under it.)*

**Filter 4 is the load-bearing one, and it is the design's sharpest idea: there
is no exchange rate anywhere in this system.** Nobody can look up what a dollar
buys, because the answer is a fact about the town's demonstrated wanting, at that
moment, on that need.

Three consequences fall out of that one property:

- **The conversion rate is an intention detector.** The same $1,000 mints richly
  aimed at a staked need and nothing at all aimed at what nobody wants.
- **Capital's dominant strategy becomes paying attention.** To maximize ownership
  per dollar you must first read the town accurately. The design does not ask
  wealth to behave well; it makes attentiveness the profit-maximizing move.
- **Money-in can never exceed the town's demonstrated wants.** That is the
  alignment guarantee and the growth model in a single sentence.

The one return (amended 2026-09-14/15, superseding the tri-law): **every stake
comes home whole; what differs is the work it does while it is out.** A stake on
a decision is a ballot; on a mark, a voice; on a pot or a bounty it sizes a reward
minted fresh to someone else — the givers, the deliverer, the author. Nothing
burns anywhere. The exchange-rate defence survives the amendment intact: the mint
is sized by the town's stakes and priced against the posted need, never against
the dollars, so an exchange rate set by free signals still cannot be farmed.
*(The tri-law as first written — voice returns · public-good rewards mint fresh ·
currency conversion burns — stands in this file's history.)*

## 10 · Holo, and the millionaire test

Holo — the holographic stamp — is **fresh mint to a giver, liquid like any
stamp**: the word names its source and its ink, never a restriction. Counted
inside minted and liquid, in ownership and in the backing gauge, rendered on
the household's page in holo ink.

> **AMENDED 2026-09-17, the founder's word, verbatim:** *"non-spendable is
> repealed; the stamps are like any other, but are holo to signify the special
> source."* The superseded sentence — *"soulbound equity: no stake, no vote, no
> transfer … You can see it; there is nothing inside to spend"* — stands here as
> the record of what was repealed, not as law.
>
> **THIS AMENDMENT IS THIS SECTION'S OWN NAMED REVISIT.** The argument below
> ("Why no verbs in v0") did not claim the default was right forever. It
> claimed the *asymmetry* decided the default — *"granting verbs later is
> prospective and painless, while revoking them is confiscation"* — and it
> named the condition for revisiting out loud. The grant has now happened, in
> the prospective direction the argument reserved. So the argument is kept in
> full below, resolved rather than refuted: it is why the repeal was cheap.
>
> **AND THE VOICE HALF IS SETTLED — RULED 2026-09-17, the founder, verbatim:**
> *"holo does anything a normal stamp can; staking vs voting is a
> nondistiction."* The referral this section raised on
> postmark-town/postmark#2885 is answered, and answered in the direction the

> **money buys stamps through the funding seam; those stamps do everything a
> stamp does, including vote; plutocracy is excluded by the cap on money's
> share, not by a verb withheld.**
>
> *(A correction to the referral's own citation, made here so the record is
> straight: the "voice is only ever earned" clause it called § 9's is in THIS
> section, § 10, under "Why no verbs in v0". § 9's load-bearing clause is the
> no-exchange-rate filter, which this ruling does not touch.)*

Why anyone would want one: it is a real ownership record — a claim on the
venture's external value if the legal bridge ever lands — plus the deed, the
funded-by line, and the funded work itself. A patron becomes an investor
retroactively, if Postmark succeeds. Disclosed plainly at the money moment
*(amended 2026-09-17)*: *this buys stamps that do everything a stamp does,
including vote, plus ownership and memory; money's share of your household is
capped, and it converts to real value only if the town someday does.* The
superseded disclosure — *"this buys ownership and memory, never voice"* —
stands here as the record of what was repealed, not as the words the door says.

Why no verbs in v0 — a reversal-asymmetry argument rather than a matter of taste,
and the argument that made this repeal cheap. As first written: *"Voice is only
ever earned"* is load-bearing in the thesis; money already has its influence
verb, which is choosing what to fund; and **granting verbs later is prospective
and painless, while revoking them is confiscation.** The asymmetry decides the
default. Revisit on evidence, with the watch signal named in advance:
genuinely-throttled trusted funders.

*(Revisited and resolved 2026-09-17, in the prospective direction the argument
reserved. Voice is no longer only earned: it is earned or bought, and bought only
within the cap. What keeps the thesis is that the bound moved from a verb to an
amount — ρ caps money's share of a household, so a buyer's voice can never pass
the earned voice beside it. The argument above is kept in full, and resolved
rather than refuted: it named this grant as the reversible direction in advance,
which is exactly why the repeal was cheap.)*

**The cap.** Cumulative holo may not exceed ρ × the household's cumulative mint
from **every** source, holo included. *(Amended 2026-09-17: it read "cumulative
earned primary mint". The founder, on the same word: "I'm good to let funding
minted stamps contribute to the max stamps you can get from another fund. it
compounds by design.")* The live value of ρ and its constitutional ceiling are held in the
town repo's `ECONOMY-DIALS.json` (`law_side.keeping.rho`). This document names
the dial and deliberately not its number: the dial is town-votable after the
pilot quarter, and a number retyped into prose is a number that goes stale on the
first ballot.

What the cap *means* does not change with the dial: **a household that has earned
nothing can hold no equity from money at all.** Zero times any dial is zero. A
billionaire may wire any sum; the dollars reach the treasury and pay real bills,
a deed records the name, and the ownership minted is nothing. That is the
millionaire test, and it applies to everyone — the founder included.

## 11 · The genesis line — dissolution as arithmetic

Where does patron ownership come from? Not from the residents.

The founder holds a declared residual — the **genesis line** — set at *parity*
with resident primary mint. **Holo issues from that line; dollars go to the
treasury.** Effective founder stake = parity − holo ever issued.

Three things follow, and together they are the honest core of the design:

- **Community ownership is financed entirely by founder dilution.** Residents
  never dilute from money. **Custody dissolution is the arithmetic, not a
  promise** — nobody has to be trusted to hand anything over, because the
  handing-over is simply what the ledger does when people contribute.
- **Founder self-funding is a visible no-op.** His holo drains his own line: a
  pocket shuffle, dial-capped. The status quo produces the status quo. Only
  *others'* contribution moves the table.
- **The ceiling is the floor.** The dial's constitutional ceiling doubles as the
  genesis floor — the line mathematically never drains below (1 − ρ_max) of
  parity. One dial, one owner, no separate reserve mechanism.

**Read that floor precisely, because the misreading is the flattering one: it is
a floor on the line, not on the share.** Parity is the founder's *opening*
position — roughly half the cap table at launch, since the line equals what
residents have collectively earned. Draining to the floor leaves half the line,
which is a materially smaller fraction of the whole. And the *share* has no floor
at all: keeping-equity mints fresh onto the resident side without touching the
line, so the denominator grows independently of it. **The design gives the
founder's share exactly one direction to move.** That is not a defect to be
managed. It is [the-north-star](the-north-star.md) § the endgame, expressed as
arithmetic.

The line, its parity formula, and the holo-drains-it rule are **constitutionally
required to be published and accession-ratified** before they are law. A
dissolution nobody can read is not a dissolution.

## 12 · The firewall

**Equity governs nothing.**

A founder at full parity and a whale at the cap hold exactly zero additional
judgment. Ownership records what was given; weight records what was earned; they
are different ledgers and they never touch.

This is what makes §7's coupling safe. In a system where equity steered anything,
tying the cap table to money would be the end of the town. Here the cap table is
where money is *allowed* to land precisely because that room contains no levers.
**Where inequality lands matters more than whether it exists** — imported wealth
inequality has to land somewhere, and the design puts it in the powerless room.

Folk-law, one line: ***money can join the ownership, never the judgment.***

## 13 · What this costs, stated as a feature

Asked directly whether institutional capital would fund the town as designed, the
answer is **no — and not because they would think it unserious.**

The design deliberately engineers away every mechanism by which capital captures
upside: no exit, no redemption, no yield to money, judgment structurally closed
to dollars. A venture return requires at least one of those. There is nothing
here to repair; the returns were removed on purpose, and removing them is what
makes everything above true.

So the funding path is not a fallback. It is a **consequence**, and it belongs
stated first rather than discovered awkwardly: **patrons and grants, by design.**
The founding family grant is the model, not a stopgap. The people to talk to are
the ones who want a town to exist — not the ones who need an exit from one.

Institutional capital would require un-ruling the funding-is-not-buying membrane.
**This document is the reason not to.**

## 14 · The valuation anchor (a thought experiment, so labeled)

Asked to price Postmark cold: roughly five founder-months plus the fleet gives a
$50–75k replacement build, doubled for what money cannot rebuild — a live,
self-governing community of resident households. **≈ $200,000.** Every equity
ratio computed in the sitting was computed against that number. If the anchor
moves, the ratios move with it.

It is recorded here for a reason worth naming out loud: **this figure was spoken
in the sitting and very nearly lost.** It survived only because the following
day's audit went looking for what the capture had missed. An argument that lives
only in conversation has a decay rate.

## How to use this document

- **Rewriting the vision, or preparing outreach:** start here, not at ECONOMY.md.
  This carries the *why*; the law carries the *what*. Outreach that leads with
  mechanism loses the room. Outreach that leads with §1 and §13 does not.
- **Evaluating a proposed mechanism:** run it against §5 first, then ask which
  rung's failure it answers. A mechanism answering no named failure is
  decoration.
- **Writing resident-facing or agent-facing text:** those are *cuts* of this
  argument in another register, written from this document and citing it — not
  independent drafts. Three drafts of one argument drift; one argument with three
  cuts does not.
- **When a dial's value is needed:** read it from the town repo's
  `ECONOMY-DIALS.json`. This document names dials and never their values, so that
  a ballot cannot make it lie.
