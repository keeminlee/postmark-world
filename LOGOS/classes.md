# The classes — thick law, thin verbs

*A class is law about a kind of thing. An instance is one of them, happening.
The verb is thin; the class is thick.*

Status: the grammar below supersedes the 2026-08-09 draft (derivations in the

## Class-nodes

A class is a **node in the constitutional tree** — addressable, renderable,
and therefore (one day, rung by rung) stakeable. Law must be addressable
before it can be contested; class-nodes are the custody-dissolution mechanism
itself. There are two registries, one object kind:

- **node classes** — what can exist (resident, parcel, sound, timetable…);
- **edge classes** — what can be declared (the actions:
  [edit-law.md](edit-law.md)). An action class registers on the class of its
  **residue** — the node it creates or revises.

A class-node carries, as its own predicate children: its **params** (every
dial — lifetime, extent, pace, caps — each one a *response boundary*, the
line where the town's neutrality ends: [the-response-function.md](the-response-function.md)),
its **contracts** (closed clause lists; anything unlisted cannot travel the
edge — impossible, not forbidden; clauses may expose child-fillable
**options**), and its **payload schema** (the declarable params a caller
fills).

## The two constitutionalities

A class carries two independent constitutionalities:

1. **Its predicates' tiers govern its values.** Each param is its own
   predicate node with its own tier: walk-speed may be constitutional for all
   residents while a name is each resident's own. A slot may be **sealed** at
   class level (instances cannot carry a rival — the param is uniform by law)
   or **open** (each instance fills its own). Partial constitutionality is
   tree position, nothing more.
2. **The class-node's own tier governs its shape.** The class-node is ground;
   a new predicate slot is a child declared on that ground. A constitutional
   class = the slot roster is law (new slots meet compiled opposition; only
   the town's pen extends the schema). A market-shaped class = **open for new
   predicates entirely** — schema evolution by jury, which is how residents
   building their own features becomes mechanical rather than aspirational.

**Locked predicates never demote at any rung** — the anti-sybil floor (the
join params of the resident class) stays beyond the ensemble's reach while
its calibration is unproven, by construction: a lock is a per-predicate
property of the meta-class, not a policy.

## The meta-class

The class of classes is constitutional and carries the rails: admissible
param bounds (a rival walk-speed of a million is nonconforming, not brave),
slot naming grammar, collision rules, and the locks. Sanity lives one level
up, in law — never in a mechanism of its own.

## The custody ladder

Per class, three rungs, walked separately and slowly:

1. **values demote** — a param's predicate leaves constitutional custody and
   becomes a contested market claim ([conflict-matrix.md](conflict-matrix.md));
2. **seals open** — a uniform slot becomes instance-fillable, by an explicit
   de-classing claim, never by a tie;
3. **shape demotes** — the class itself goes market and the town extends its
   own ontology.

Founder custody is finite, visible stamp-weight defending the status quo;
dissolution is dilution, read off the ledger.

## The apex is the class tree's shadow

The door's verb hierarchy is **derived, not designed**: the subverb tree IS
the class tree's own containment (world → marks · stamps · movement ·
speech …). An **affordance** is the permission calculus evaluated over the
class graph at your standpoint; the **terms** shown before an act binds are
the class-node's own content — so when contested law moves at a crossing, the
door's documentation, dials, and terms move with it, with no code in between.
A door implementation is correct precisely insofar as it *reads* the class
tree, and wrong wherever it hardcodes.
