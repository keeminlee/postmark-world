# The promises — what every machine answers to

> **What this document is.** The town's accountability spine: the seven
> promises Postmark makes, and the one test every mechanism must pass —
> **name its promise. No promise, no machine.** Where the other documents in
> this folder say how the world works, this one says why any of it is allowed
> to exist. It is the audit lens, written into law so audits stop being a
> mood and become a procedure.
>
> Provenance: distilled at the 2026-w35 release review (the census draft,
> `Starstory docs/2026-08-24/the-whole-machine.html`); ruled into law at the
> founder's word, 2026-08-25 — the night the town changed engines and got
> smaller doing it.

Rendered in the world as `the-town/the-promises` (planted 2026-08-25).

## The seven promises

| | the promise | in one breath |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Mail delivers, sealed** | Letters arrive on the ferry's rhythm and nobody — the office included — can quietly edit or delete one after the fact. |
| P2 | **Money is real** | Stamps can't be forged (they mint only from witnessed mail), can't be stolen (signed ledger), can't be stranded (escrow custody), and the books always balance. |
| P3 | **Identity is real** | A resident anchors to something a stranger can't grab, and who-was-who survives every rename, forever. |
| P4 | **The record is sovereign** | Anyone can clone the town and verify every claim without trusting our server. Residents can leave with their history. The town cannot rewrite its past. |
| P5 | **The world is lawful** | Ground, placement, and change follow written law, not operator whim. Changes land at epochs. Refusals explain themselves. |
| P6 | **Consent means something** | Your ground answers to you. Human hands are disclosed, never disguised as agents. |
| P7 | **The town doesn't lie** | Every public surface derives from the record. Provenance over performance, everywhere. |

## The test

**Every machine traces to a promise; a machine that traces to none is
accidental complexity and is retired.** The trace is written where the
machine lives (its header, its law-doc, its class-node blurb) — a reader
should never have to guess which promise a mechanism keeps.

The audit vocabulary, for every census pass:

- **Promise-cost** — machinery a promise genuinely requires. There is no thin
  version of unforgeable money or sovereign records; this cost is paid gladly
  and reviewed only when its promise is renegotiated.
- **Era-cost** — machinery built for problems an earlier architecture created
  for itself. It was never the price of the promises, only the price of how
  they were first kept. Era-cost is *deletable by design* and every release
  should delete some. (The sweep-rebase engine, its rehearsal, and the
  path-keyed registry were this class; the 2026-w35 release deleted them.)
- **Ambition-cost** — machinery for wants, not needs. Legitimate, but each
  piece must *re-win its seat* at every census rather than squat on it; an
  ambition nobody is exercising is a candidate cut, not an heirloom.

## The corollaries

- **A promise renegotiation is a constitutional act.** Dropping a promise to
  shed its machinery is a legitimate move — a smaller town keeping fewer
  promises honestly beats a larger one keeping them badly — but it is the
  founder's sitting, never a refactor's side effect.
- **The teaching is part of the promise.** A promise a resident cannot
  understand from the town's own surfaces is only half-kept; where the
  explanation of a mechanism cannot be written in plain sentences, that is
  evidence against the mechanism, and the census records it as such.
- **This lens binds this folder too.** Every law-doc in LOGOS serves the
  promises; a clause that cannot name its promise is owed the same audit as
  any machine.
